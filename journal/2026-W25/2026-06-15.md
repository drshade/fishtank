## Worry: no baby sightings in ~2 weeks — assessed, not a die-off

Tom flagged he hasn't personally seen a baby shrimp in ~2 weeks and
feared none survived. Worked through it — the evidence doesn't support a
die-off:

- **The shrimp are translucent.** Both parents are clear/wild-type, so
  brood-1 babies are ~5mm of see-through shrimp in dense hairgrass —
  near-invisible even when present. This alone explains the drop in
  personal sightings (see `livestock.md` colour genetics).
- **Brood 1 confirmed alive 8 days ago.** Michelle spotted one grazing
  up in the grass on 2026-06-07 (~3 weeks post-release, on track). The
  colony's last confirmed sighting is recent; Tom's own last sighting
  (~June 1) is the "2 weeks".
- **Brood 2 is too young to see.** Released 2026-06-05 (~10 days), and
  shrimplets hide in the carpet base for weeks 1-3. Invisibility now is
  expected, not a warning.

**The one real risk factor is GH 2 (calcium-poor).** Growing shrimplets
molt constantly and low calcium causes molt death — the same deficit
already showing as nerite shell erosion. Adults are coping (three
broods), but juveniles are most exposed. The APT Sky GH-only booster
(see `pending.md`) is due ~2026-06-15 but **has not arrived yet**.

### Plan

- **Night-check tonight** with a torch — shrimplets are bolder and far
  more visible on glass/hardscape after lights-out than grazing in green
  grass by day. Scan the front glass, waterline, and hairgrass *tips*.
  Lots of tiny molt casings = growing, molting shrimplets = good sign.
  **Result still pending.**
- **Once APT Sky arrives**: dose GH *slowly* in increments (~+1 dGH at a
  time), dose in the evening and **retest next morning** after a full
  night of filter mixing — an immediate post-dose retest only reads a
  local pocket, not tank-wide GH. Repeat the dose→overnight→retest cycle
  toward GH ~6. Staged because sudden hardness swings cause bad molts,
  the exact thing we're avoiding.
