# Care Knowledge — this tank, settled decisions

Things that have been thought through and decided. Don't re-litigate
these without good reason; if a decision changes, update the entry
*and* prepend a note to `timeline.md`.

## Shrimplets (first 4-6 weeks)

- Neocaridina are born as fully-formed miniatures, no larval stage.
  Their first 2-3 weeks they graze biofilm and microfauna — no
  supplemental feeding needed. Dense hairgrass and lava rocks are
  their pasture.
- They hide in the hairgrass base for weeks 1-3. **Not seeing them ≠
  dead.** They reappear at ~5mm body length.
- Per-individual they're *more* O2-tolerant than adults (lower bioload).
- Survival 70-90% in healthy tanks; some loss is normal biology.
- Colour genetics appear ~week 3-4.

### What kills shrimplets

In order of how often it actually happens:
1. **Accidental siphoning** during carpet maintenance — by far #1
2. **Big water changes** with mismatched parameters
3. **Ammonia spike** from over-feeding or disturbed filter
4. **Sudden temperature change**
5. **CO2 overshoot at night** (poor surface agitation, no aeration)
6. Intake suction (only with unprotected slot-style intakes — solved
   by the stainless mesh filter guard)

### Carpet siphoning rule (while babies < ~4-6 weeks)

- **Don't** push the siphon into the hairgrass
- **Do** hover above the carpet, stir gently from above with a chopstick
  to suspend mulm, then siphon the cloud
- **Always** siphon into a clear container, inspect for 10 min before
  disposing — catch accidental shrimplet pickups and pour them back

## Adult breeding cycle

- Female molts ~24-48h after releasing a brood; mating happens
  immediately post-molt while shell is soft. Re-berrying within 24-72h
  of release is *normal*, not a problem.
- Brood cycle: ~25-30 days carry → release ~20-30 babies → repeat.
- One female can produce 300+ shrimp in a 1.5-2 year lifespan.
- One male can fertilise many females → inbreeding pressure builds
  over generations → refresh genetics every 6-12 months.

## CO2 and oxygen — the big one

- **Don't turn CO2 off "for safety".** This is a known bad move:
  destabilises plants, triggers algae outbreak (stressed plants lose
  to algae for nutrients), and does *nothing* to fix the real risk.
- Real O2 risk window: ~4am, pre-dawn. Plants have been respiring all
  night, no photosynthesis, surface agitation is the only O2 source.
- **The fix is surface agitation, not CO2 reduction.**
- Current CO2 timing (off ~1h before lights-off) is correct. Plants
  finish photosynthesising on residual CO2, then transition cleanly.
- Lily pipe outflow positioned just below waterline, angled along long
  axis of tank = constant ripple + no splashing + no CO2 off-gassing
  during the day. This is the ideal.

## Aeration decision (settled 2026-05-22)

Tom decided to add a Tetra AirSilent Mini air pump despite preferring
no extra equipment, *after* the dawn near-miss. Reasoning:

- He watches the tank ~8h/day from his desk during work hours, which is
  genuine continuous monitoring most keepers don't have
- BUT: O2 dips happen pre-dawn when he's not watching
- AND: the canister filter is a single point of failure once installed
  (impeller stall = no flow = no surface aeration)
- Air pump scheduled inverse to CO2 (night-only via smart plug) gives
  cheap, independent redundancy

Once tuned (flow valve + finer airstone), expected to be near-invisible
in aesthetic terms.

## Filter maintenance

- **Sluggish filter = O2 risk.** Clean when flow drops, not on a
  calendar.
- **Squeeze media in tank water, never tap.** Chlorine kills the
  nitrifying bacteria you're trying to keep.
- **Parallel-run when swapping filters.** 2-3 weeks minimum. Seed the
  new media with a chunk of old sponge from day one.
- For canister cleans (post-transition): only when flow noticeably
  drops. Each open disturbs colonised media.

## Water changes — settled approach

- Small (10-15%, ~2L), slow refill, temperature- and parameter-matched.
- Pause for 2-3 weeks after a new brood.
- **Stability > freshness.** Lots of established shrimp keepers do
  small weekly changes their whole career and never crash — the trick
  is consistency, not volume.

## Detritus and algae

- **Biofilm on glass/rocks** = shrimp food, mostly leave it alone.
- **Wispy "spider web" stuff** that breaks into dust: detritus +
  bacterial biofilm webs. Looks alarming, isn't. Will dissipate when
  flow reaches that area (canister intake placement will fix the
  death corner).
- **Smell test**:
  - Musty / swampy = cyanobacteria (BGA) — treat carefully, antibiotics
    risky with shrimplets, blackout + flow fix is safer
  - Rotten egg / sulfur = anaerobic substrate pocket — gentle stir
  - Neutral / earthy / sand-bark = normal mulm + biofilm, no action
- **Algae in dead zones** is a flow problem, not an algae problem.
  Fix the flow.

## TDS pen — useful proxy

- Once Tom has one, a TDS measurement summarises overall water
  chemistry stability in one number
- Climbing = minerals accumulating → water change
- Dropping = molts pulling minerals faster than replaced →
  remineralise top-off water
- Target 150-250 ppm for neos

## Equipment aesthetic principles (Tom's stated preferences)

- Removing in-tank equipment is the real aesthetic win (heater,
  internal filter) — not buying nicer in-tank equipment
- Stainless mesh filter guard hides better than foam pre-filter sponge
- Flow control valves matter more for noise than fancy stones
- 10mm glass (FiltoSmart 60-class) hides better in a 20L than 13mm,
  but Tom went 13mm to get the canister thermo, the larger glassware
  selection, and future flexibility — defensible trade

## Future risk thresholds

When any of these trigger, revisit the corresponding decision:

- **Colony 30-40 visible juveniles** → nighttime airstone goes from
  "optional insurance" to "strongly recommended"
- **Summer (Amsterdam apartments hit 26-28°C in August without AC)** →
  warm water holds less O2; revisit aeration headroom
- **Tom going away >1 day** → no on-site monitoring; airstone becomes
  load-bearing
- **Heater fail mode** (canister thermo unique failure: power loss kills
  both flow and heat simultaneously) → acceptable for Amsterdam climate
  + 20L thermal mass, neos tolerate 18-25°C
